🧪 Interpretation and limitations

Key finding. The previous indoor PM₂.₅ reading is a powerful next-bin predictor. Persistence achieved the lowest average absolute error, while gradient boosting reduced larger errors as reflected by RMSE.

The 133 held-out observations at or above 30 µg/m³ were rare; LightGBM had smaller MAE than persistence in this observed-concentration category, but elevated-event detection was not evaluated prospectively.

Scope and caveats. PM₂.₅ was capped at 100 µg/m³; the task is 10-minute prediction rather than one-hour forecasting. The held-out homes were previously examined during earlier experimentation, so the results are retrospective. The XGBoost results came from an additional tuning run on the same data partition. Feature importance is not a causal measurement.
